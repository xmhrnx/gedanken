In 1915 most astronomers thought the universe was the Milky Way, static and perhaps eternal. Within fifteen years they learned that the faint spiral "nebulae" were galaxies like our own, millions of light-years away, and that almost all of them are moving apart.

## Redshifts

Light from a source moving away from us arrives stretched to longer wavelengths, toward the red. Astronomers measure this from the dark absorption lines in a spectrum, whose laboratory wavelengths are known. The **redshift** z is the fractional stretch.

::: equation Redshift, and speed for nearby galaxies
z = \frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{obs}} - \lambda_{\mathrm{emit}}}{\lambda_{\mathrm{emit}}}, \quad v \approx cz
:::

The second formula, speed from redshift, holds only when z is much less than 1.

::: history 1912–1917 · Flagstaff, Arizona
Vesto Slipher at Lowell Observatory spent long nights exposing photographic plates to record the spectra of spiral nebulae. In 1912 he found Andromeda approaching at about 300 km/s, but by 1917 most of the 25 spirals he had measured were receding, some at over 1000 km/s, far faster than any star. No one yet knew how far away they were.
:::

Distances came from **Cepheid variables**, stars that pulse with a period tied to their true brightness, a relation Henrietta Leavitt found in 1912. Comparing a Cepheid's true brightness with how bright it looks gives its distance. In 1924 Edwin Hubble found Cepheids in Andromeda and showed it lay far outside the Milky Way.

## A law of recession

::: history 1927 · Louvain, Belgium
Georges Lemaître, a Belgian priest and physicist, found that Einstein's equations of general relativity allow a universe that expands, as Alexander Friedmann had shown in 1922. Using Slipher's speeds and rough distances he predicted that recession speed should grow in proportion to distance, and estimated the rate. He published in a little-read Belgian journal, in French. Einstein told him, "Your calculations are correct, but your physics is abominable."
:::

In 1929 Hubble, using Slipher's redshifts and his own distances, published a plot of speed against distance for 24 galaxies. The points scattered but rose in a straight line. In 2018 the International Astronomical Union recommended calling the result the Hubble–Lemaître law.

::: equation The Hubble–Lemaître law
v = H_0 d
:::

H₀, the **Hubble constant**, is today's expansion rate. Astronomers quote it in kilometres per second per megaparsec (1 Mpc, a megaparsec, is about 3.26 million light-years). Hubble got about 500 km/s/Mpc; his Cepheid calibration was badly off, and later work brought it down to about 70. Each megaparsec of distance adds about 70 km/s of recession speed.

::: steps From a spectrum to a distance
$ \lambda_{\mathrm{emit}} = 656.3\,\text{nm}, \quad \lambda_{\mathrm{obs}} = 663.0\,\text{nm}
A hydrogen line, red in the laboratory, observed in a galaxy's spectrum.
---
$ z = \frac{663.0 - 656.3}{656.3} \approx 0.0102
The fractional stretch, about 1%.
---
$ v \approx cz = 3.00 \times 10^5\,\text{km/s} \times 0.0102 \approx 3060\,\text{km/s}
At such a small redshift the Doppler formula is accurate enough.
---
$ d = \frac{v}{H_0} \approx \frac{3060\,\text{km/s}}{70\,\text{km/s/Mpc}} \approx 44\,\text{Mpc}
About 140 million light-years. For nearby galaxies the galaxy's own motion, a few hundred km/s, adds an error of up to about 10%.
:::

## What is expanding

The law does not put us at the centre. If every distance grows by the same factor, every observer sees every other galaxy receding at a speed proportional to its distance. The one analogy worth making is a loaf of raisin bread rising in the oven: from any raisin, the others move away, and those twice as far move twice as fast.

In general relativity the galaxies are not flying through space; space itself is stretching, carrying them along, and the wavelength of light stretches with it. The cosmological redshift is a measure of how much the universe has grown while the light travelled: 1 + z is the ratio of the universe's size now to its size when the light left. Gravity holds galaxies, stars and atoms together against this, so they do not expand.

## The Hubble tension

Two precise methods now disagree. The cosmic microwave background, interpreted with the standard cosmological model, gives H₀ ≈ 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc. The distance ladder, Cepheids calibrating Type Ia supernovae, gives about 73 ± 1. The gap of roughly 8% has survived checks with the James Webb Space Telescope, though some teams using other stars as calibrators get values near 70. Either some measurement has a hidden error, or the standard model of the universe is missing something.

::: note
Distant galaxies recede at speeds proportional to their distance, v = H₀d with H₀ ≈ 70 km/s/Mpc. Every observer sees the same law, because space itself is expanding.
:::
