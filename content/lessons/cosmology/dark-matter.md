Astronomers weigh things in space by watching how they move. A planet's orbit gives the Sun's mass; a star's orbit gave the mass of the black hole at the galaxy's centre. Applied to galaxies and clusters of galaxies, the same method gives a startling answer: they contain far more mass than all their stars and gas.

## Zwicky's missing mass

::: history 1933 · Pasadena, California
Fritz Zwicky measured the speeds of galaxies in the Coma Cluster, a swarm of about a thousand galaxies some 100 Mpc away. They moved around each other at about 1000 km/s. For the cluster to hold together at those speeds, its gravity required hundreds of times more mass than its galaxies' light suggested. He called the missing ingredient *dunkle Materie*, dark matter. His factor was inflated by the wrong Hubble constant of the day, and much of the shortfall was later found as hot gas, but a large gap remains.
:::

For forty years the result was largely set aside; clusters were complicated, and no one was sure they were stable. The case that convinced most astronomers came from single galaxies.

## Flat rotation curves

A spiral galaxy is a rotating disc. Its **rotation curve** is the orbital speed of stars and gas plotted against distance from the centre, measured from Doppler shifts on the side turning toward us and the side turning away.

::: history 1970s · Washington, D.C.
Vera Rubin and Kent Ford, at the Carnegie Institution, used a sensitive new spectrograph to measure the rotation of Andromeda (1970) and then dozens of other spirals. The curves did not fall off at the edge of the visible disc; they stayed flat. Radio astronomers tracking hydrogen gas far beyond the stars, notably Morton Roberts and Albert Bosma, found the same.
:::

What should a rotation curve look like? Newton's gravity gives a direct answer.

::: steps The flat-rotation-curve argument
$ \frac{v^2}{r} = \frac{G M(r)}{r^2}
A star on a circular orbit of radius r needs centripetal acceleration v²/r, supplied by gravity. M(r) is the mass inside radius r; for a roughly spherical distribution the mass outside pulls equally in all directions and cancels, as Newton's shell theorem shows. A flat disc needs a correction of order tens of percent, not a change in the conclusion.
---
$ v = \sqrt{\frac{G M(r)}{r}}
Multiply both sides by r and take the square root.
---
$ M(r) \approx \text{constant} \Rightarrow v \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}
Most of the light is concentrated toward the centre. If the mass follows the light, then beyond the bright disc M(r) stops growing and speeds should fall, as the planets' speeds fall with distance from the Sun.
---
$ v \approx \text{constant} \Rightarrow M(r) = \frac{v^2 r}{G} \propto r
The observed speeds stay flat. Solving the first line for M(r) shows that the enclosed mass must keep growing in proportion to r, far beyond where the light runs out.
---
$ M = \frac{(2.2 \times 10^5\,\text{m/s})^2 \times 2.5 \times 10^{20}\,\text{m}}{6.67 \times 10^{-11}\,\text{m}^3\,\text{kg}^{-1}\,\text{s}^{-2}} \approx 1.8 \times 10^{41}\,\text{kg}
For the Milky Way, v ≈ 220 km/s at the Sun's distance of about 8 kpc (2.5 × 10²⁰ m). That is about 9 × 10¹⁰ solar masses inside the Sun's orbit, and the curve stays flat much further out.
:::

The conclusion is that each galaxy sits inside a much larger, roughly spherical **halo** of matter that emits no light and outweighs the stars several times over.

## Lensing and the Bullet Cluster

General relativity says mass bends light. A massive cluster acts as a lens, distorting the images of galaxies behind it into arcs and stretched shapes. Measuring the distortion maps the total mass, visible or not, without assuming anything about how the cluster moves. Lensing masses agree with the dynamical ones: clusters hold about five to six times as much dark matter as ordinary matter.

::: history 2006 · the Bullet Cluster
Douglas Clowe and colleagues studied 1E 0657-56, two galaxy clusters that passed through each other about 150 million years ago. Most of a cluster's ordinary mass is hot gas, seen in X-rays by the Chandra telescope. The gas clouds collided and were slowed, and now sit between the two groups of galaxies. Lensing showed that most of the mass had passed straight through with the galaxies.
:::

The mass and the ordinary matter are in different places. That is hard to explain by changing the law of gravity, as theories such as MOND (Mordehai Milgrom, 1983) try to do, and natural if most of the mass is particles that barely interact, even with each other.

## What is it?

Dark matter is not ordinary matter in dark form: the deuterium made in the Big Bang and the microwave background both fix the amount of ordinary matter, and it is about one sixth of the total matter. Neutrinos are too light and fast to form galaxy-sized clumps. Leading candidates are new particles: weakly interacting massive particles (WIMPs), or very light **axions**. Large underground detectors have searched for decades and found nothing yet, steadily ruling out the simplest WIMP models.

::: note
Stars far from a galaxy's centre orbit as fast as those near it. By v = √(GM(r)/r), that means the enclosed mass keeps growing where there is no light. Lensing and the Bullet Cluster confirm that most of the universe's matter is invisible.
:::
