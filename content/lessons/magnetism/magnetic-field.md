Lodestone, a naturally magnetic iron ore, was known in ancient Greece and China, and by about 1200 sailors in both China and Europe steered by magnetized needles. For most of history magnetism looked like a separate force from electricity, with its own rules. The central discovery of this chapter is that it is not: magnetic fields are made by moving charges.

## Poles

Every magnet has two ends, called **north** and **south poles** after the direction a freely hanging magnet points. Like poles repel and unlike poles attract, which sounds just like electric charge. But there is a crucial difference. Cut a bar magnet in half and you do not get a separate north and south: you get two smaller magnets, each with both poles. Keep cutting down to single atoms and the result is the same. No isolated magnetic pole, or **monopole**, has ever been found, despite careful searches.

::: history 1269 · Lucera, Italy
Petrus Peregrinus, a French scholar serving in an army besieging the town, wrote a letter describing experiments with a lodestone sphere. He mapped the directions a needle took on its surface and found they converged on two points, which he named poles. He noted that like poles repel, unlike attract, and that breaking a magnet gives two complete magnets.
:::

::: history 1600 · London
William Gilbert, physician to Elizabeth I, published *De Magnete*. Using a small lodestone sphere he called a *terrella*, "little Earth", he showed that a compass needle on it behaves as a compass does on Earth, including dipping toward the surface near the poles. He concluded that the Earth itself is a giant magnet. This replaced older ideas that compasses were drawn by the Pole Star or by magnetic mountains in the Arctic.
:::

Because a magnet's north pole is attracted to geographic north, the Earth's magnetic pole in the Arctic is, strictly, a magnetic *south* pole. Its position drifts by tens of kilometres a year.

## The magnetic field

As with electricity, it is cleanest to describe magnetism by a field filling space. The **magnetic field** B at a point has the direction a small compass needle would point there, and a strength measured in **teslas** (T). The tesla is large:

| Source | Field |
|---|---|
| Earth's surface | 25–65 μT |
| Fridge magnet | about 5 mT |
| Strong neodymium magnet, at its surface | about 1 T |
| Hospital MRI scanner | 1.5–3 T |

Magnetic field lines can be revealed by sprinkling iron filings around a magnet: each filing becomes a tiny compass and lines up with the field. Outside a bar magnet the lines run from north pole to south. Unlike electric field lines, they do not stop there: they continue through the magnet from south back to north, forming closed loops. That is the field-line way of saying there are no monopoles. In equation form, Gauss's law for magnetism says the net number of field lines leaving any closed surface is zero: ∇·B = 0.

## Moving charge makes magnetic field

In 1820 it was discovered that an electric current deflects a compass, as the next lessons describe. Measurements soon gave the field around a long straight wire carrying current I: the lines are circles around the wire, and the field strength at distance r is

::: equation Field of a long straight wire
B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}, \qquad \mu_0 \approx 4\pi \times 10^{-7}\,\text{T m/A}
:::

The constant μ₀ is the **permeability of free space**. For a household current of 10 A, at 1 cm from the wire, B = (2 × 10⁻⁷ × 10)/0.01 = 2 × 10⁻⁴ T, about four times the Earth's field. That is why a compass near a wire can mislead.

Every magnetic field ever measured is produced by moving charge, or by a property of particles that behaves like it. So where are the currents in a permanent magnet? Inside atoms. Electrons have an intrinsic magnetism, called **spin**, and their motion around nuclei contributes too; a full explanation needs quantum mechanics. In most materials these atomic magnets point in random directions and cancel. In iron, nickel and cobalt, neighbouring atoms line up in regions called **domains**, and in a magnetized piece the domains themselves are aligned.

::: note
Magnetic fields come from moving charges, from currents in wires down to electrons in atoms. Their field lines always close on themselves: there are no magnetic monopoles.
:::

## Electricity and magnetism are one thing

Einstein showed in 1905 that the split between electric and magnetic fields depends on how you are moving. A charge at rest in your frame produces only an electric field; to someone moving past it, the same charge is a current, and they also measure a magnetic field. What one observer calls a magnetic force another calls partly electric. The two are aspects of a single **electromagnetic field**.
