The electroweak theory had a flaw at its centre. Its symmetry, the one that forces the photon, W and Z to exist, also forbids them from having mass. Writing a mass term into the equations breaks the symmetry and makes predictions at high energy nonsensical. Yet the W and Z are heavy, and so are the electron and the quarks, whose masses are forbidden by the same symmetry. The way out was proposed in 1964 and confirmed 48 years later.

## A field that is not zero

::: history 1964 · Brussels, Edinburgh and London
Three groups published the same mechanism within months: François Englert and Robert Brout; Peter Higgs; and Gerald Guralnik, Carl Hagen and Tom Kibble. Higgs added the remark that it predicted a new massive particle. Weinberg and Salam built the mechanism into the electroweak theory in 1967–68.
:::

The idea is that space is filled with a new field, the **Higgs field**, whose lowest-energy state is not zero. Every other field is zero in empty space; this one has a constant value everywhere, even in a perfect vacuum. The equations remain symmetric, but the state the universe sits in does not. A pencil balanced on its point is symmetric; once it falls it points in one direction, though the laws did not prefer any.

The shape of the field's energy makes this happen. Treat the field as a single number φ, which keeps the essential maths.

::: steps Where the Higgs field settles
$ V(\phi) = -\tfrac{1}{2}\mu^2\phi^2 + \tfrac{1}{4}\lambda\phi^4
The energy density of the field, with μ² and λ positive constants. The negative first term makes φ = 0 a hilltop rather than a valley.
---
$ \frac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}\phi} = -\mu^2\phi + \lambda\phi^3 = 0
The lowest-energy state is where the slope is zero.
---
$ \phi = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad \phi = v = \frac{\mu}{\sqrt{\lambda}}
φ = 0 is the top of the hill and unstable. The field settles at v instead; the measured value is v ≈ 246 GeV.
---
$ V''(v) = -\mu^2 + 3\lambda v^2 = 2\mu^2
The second derivative, the curvature of the valley floor, using λv² = μ². A ripple in the field about v is a particle, and the curvature fixes that particle's mass.
---
$ m_H = \sqrt{2}\,\mu = \sqrt{2\lambda}\, v
In units where ħ = c = 1, the mass squared equals that curvature. This is the **Higgs boson**. The theory does not predict λ, so it does not predict the mass; it had to be found by experiment.
:::

Particles get mass by interacting with the constant field. The W and Z interact through the weak force and get masses fixed by its strength g: m_W = gv/2 ≈ 0.65 × 246/2 ≈ 80 GeV, as measured. Quarks and charged leptons each have their own coupling y, giving m = yv/√2. These couplings are free parameters: the top quark's is almost exactly 1, the electron's about 3 × 10⁻⁶. The mechanism explains how fermions can have mass without breaking the symmetry, but not why the masses are what they are. The photon does not interact with the field and stays massless.

Popular accounts often compare the field to molasses, but drag slows things down, whereas mass is resistance to any change in motion, so the analogy misleads.

Most of the mass of ordinary matter does not come from the Higgs. The up and down quarks in a proton have masses of a few MeV, about 1% of the proton's 938 MeV. The rest is the energy of the gluon field and the quarks' motion, through E = mc².

## Finding it

A Higgs boson lives about 10⁻²² s, so it is identified only by what it decays into. It is made in only about one in a billion proton collisions, and most of its decays look like ordinary background.

::: history 4 July 2012 · CERN, Geneva
The Large Hadron Collider, a 27 km ring that collides protons at energies of several TeV, had two general-purpose detectors, ATLAS and CMS, each run by about 3000 physicists. At a seminar that morning both reported a new boson near 125 GeV, at the five-standard-deviation level of confidence that particle physicists require, a chance of under one in a million of a fluke. Higgs, then 83, wiped away tears in the audience. Englert and Higgs received the 2013 Nobel Prize; Brout had died in 2011.
:::

The clearest signals were rare decays into two photons and into four charged leptons (via two Z bosons), which stand out as a sharp peak in the combined energy of the products. The measured mass is about 125 GeV, roughly 133 proton masses. Since 2012 its spin (zero, as predicted), its decays to bottom quarks, tau leptons and W and Z pairs, and its couplings to the heaviest particles have all been measured, and all agree with the Standard Model within present precision.

::: note
The Higgs field has a nonzero value everywhere. Particles that interact with it gain mass in proportion to the strength of that interaction, without breaking the symmetry of the equations; the Higgs boson is a ripple in that field, found at 125 GeV.
:::
