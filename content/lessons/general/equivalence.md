Special relativity rewrote space and time but left gravity out. Newton's gravity acts instantly across any distance, which clashes with a universal speed limit. Einstein spent eight years looking for a better theory, and the whole search grew from one observation that everyone since Galileo had known and nobody had taken seriously: everything falls the same way.

## Two kinds of mass

In Newton's physics, mass plays two separate roles. **Inertial mass** measures how hard an object is to accelerate: it is the m in F = ma. **Gravitational mass** measures how strongly gravity pulls on it: it is the m in Newton's law of gravity. Nothing in Newton's theory says these must be the same number. They just are, as far as anyone can measure.

::: steps Why everything falls alike
$ m_i\,a = F
Newton's second law, with m_i the inertial mass: the force needed for a given acceleration.
---
$ F = \frac{GMm_g}{r^2} = m_g\,g
The force of gravity near a body of mass M is proportional to the gravitational mass m_g. Near Earth's surface GM/r² is the familiar g ≈ 9.8 m/s².
---
$ m_i\,a = m_g\,g
The only force on a falling object is gravity, so the two expressions for F are equal.
---
$ a = \frac{m_g}{m_i}\,g
Solve for the acceleration. If the ratio m_g/m_i differed between, say, lead and wood, they would fall at different rates.
---
$ \frac{m_g}{m_i} = 1 \Rightarrow a = g
Experiment finds the same acceleration for every material, so the ratio is the same for everything, and units are chosen to make it 1.
:::

For Newton this was a coincidence to be measured. Loránd Eötvös, using a torsion balance in Budapest around 1900, showed the two masses agree to a few parts in a billion. The MICROSCOPE satellite, comparing titanium and platinum in orbit, brought it to about one part in 10¹⁵ by 2022.

## The elevator

Einstein turned the coincidence into a principle. Picture yourself in a closed, windowless box.

- If the box sits on Earth, a dropped ball falls to the floor at 9.8 m/s², and you feel your weight on your feet.
- If the box is in deep space, far from any planet, and a rocket accelerates it "upward" at 9.8 m/s², the floor rushes up to meet a released ball at 9.8 m/s², and the floor presses on your feet exactly as before.

Every ball, whatever it is made of, behaves identically in both cases, precisely because m_g = m_i. Run it the other way: a box falling freely near Earth is indistinguishable from a box floating in empty space. Everything inside floats together, which is why astronauts in orbit are weightless. They are not beyond gravity; they and their station are falling around the Earth at the same rate.

::: note
The equivalence principle: in a small enough region, the effects of gravity are exactly the same as the effects of being in an accelerating frame. No experiment done inside the box, mechanical or otherwise, can tell the two apart.
:::

Einstein's step was the "or otherwise". He assumed the equivalence holds not just for falling balls but for all of physics, including light and clocks.

::: history 1907 · Bern
Einstein was still an examiner at the Swiss patent office when he was asked to write a review of relativity. While writing it, he later recalled, he was struck by "the happiest thought of my life": a person falling freely from a roof would not feel their own weight. In that 1907 review he stated the equivalence of gravity and acceleration and already drew two predictions from it, that clocks run at different rates at different heights and that gravity bends light. It took him until 1915 to build a full theory around the idea.
:::

## Light in the box

Shine a horizontal beam across the accelerating box. While the light crosses, the floor speeds up beneath it, so to someone inside, the beam curves downward and hits the far wall slightly low. By the equivalence principle, the same must happen in a box resting on Earth: gravity bends light.

The effect is minute. Light crosses a 3 m box in 10⁻⁸ s, during which it "falls" ½gt² ≈ 5 × 10⁻¹⁶ m, less than the width of a proton. Near the Sun it becomes measurable, and a later lesson follows that story.

## Only locally

The box must be small. On a real planet, gravity points toward the centre, so two balls released side by side in a falling box drift slowly toward each other, and a ball above another drifts away from it. These **tidal effects** are real gravity that no choice of accelerating frame can remove. They are the signature of what Einstein would identify as curved spacetime, the subject of the next lesson.

::: model falling
All objects fall with the same pattern of spacing, whatever they are made of. To someone falling alongside, the ball would hang motionless.
:::
