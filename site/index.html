What it is

A physics course that shows its working.

Gedanken, German for “thoughts”, as in the Gedankenexperiment: the thought experiments that physics was built on.

Gedanken is a course in physics for your phone, written for an intelligent adult who wants to understand how physicists reached each idea: not a specialist, not a child. Every lesson sets up the problem before solving it, explains the reasoning rather than only the result, and tells the history as a story of who was stuck on what.

The equations are real, typeset mathematics, and the derivations unfold one step at a time, each with a paragraph saying what just happened. Interactive models let you push on the ideas, and a short quiz at the end of a lesson lets you check yourself, or skip it.

Finished lessons are remembered on the device, so the home screen shows how far you have come.

Inside a lesson

Six kinds of block, one careful voice.

Prose

Connected argument

Paragraphs that lead into each other, with every technical term defined the first time it appears.

Equation

Typeset mathematics

Displayed formulas written in LaTeX and drawn in STIX Two Math, with a caption saying what they are.

v = √(2GM / r)

Derivation

Step by step

Each step is an equation and an explanation. Reveal them with Next step, or show them all at once.

History

Who, where, why

Asides tagged with a year and a place: 1783 in Thornhill, 1935 at the Royal Astronomical Society.

Interactive

Models to play with

Sliders and live graphics: shrink a star until light can’t leave, or set k and watch a chain reaction grow.

Check yourself

Quizzes and key ideas

Multiple-choice questions with an explanation for the answer, and highlighted notes for the idea to keep.

Screens

Deep space, set in a book face.

A dark, night-blue reading screen; titles and reading text in STIX Two Text, each part of the course in its own colour.
